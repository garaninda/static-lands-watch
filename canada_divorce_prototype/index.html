The financial reality after separation

Divorce Changes More Than a Relationship. It Changes the Cost of Everyday Life.

When a household separates, many expenses do not disappear. They reorganize around two homes, new responsibilities and, often, one income carrying much more of the load.

The expenses don’t disappear. They reorganize around one income.
1 Before separation
  • Shared housing costs
  • Shared utilities and transportation
  • Combined emergency cushion
  • Two adults absorbing the unexpected
2 After separation
  • Separate housing and utility costs
  • Legal, moving and setup expenses
  • Childcare and transportation between homes
  • One paycheque carrying more responsibility

Participant story · composite draft

“I Wasn’t Broke. I Was Just One Expense Away From Panicking.”

My name is Karen. I’m 47 and work in administration at a dental clinic outside Calgary.

When my marriage ended after 18 years, I stayed in the house so the children wouldn’t have to change schools. On paper, I was doing okay. I had a full-time job. The fridge was full. The kids still had hockey, school trips and birthday parties.

But every month felt like a balancing act. I wasn’t falling behind. I just couldn’t afford for anything to go wrong.

I thought about taking a second job, then looked at my week. Evenings were homework, laundry, driving the kids around and trying to keep life feeling normal. I didn’t have more hours to sell.

“I didn’t need someone to rescue me. I needed a way to begin moving forward on my own.”

I took my time, asked questions and started by learning how the platform worked. The biggest change was not luxury. It was feeling that I had begun thinking about my own future again.
